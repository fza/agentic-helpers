When several commits pass the landed-commit scan's match tests for one todo, todo item or trap, the scan reports the one with the highest score `0.8 × strength + 0.2 × merge`, where `merge` is 1 for a merge commit and 0 otherwise, the newer commit winning a tie. This replaces the merge-precedence paragraph of 20261101-112431-d-tac-9t4, under which a merge match always beat a normal one; every other part of that directive, and of 20261101-110914-d-tac-pu1, stands.

**Strength, 0 to 1.** A linked graph id named in the message, or the entry's branch named by the merge or a `merged:` end: 1.0. Subject similarity: the embedder's score stretched to 0..1 above the match threshold (`(score - threshold) / (1 - threshold)`), since raw scores cluster in a narrow band where any fixed bonus would decide alone. A commit changing a path the entry is anchored to (a path its title or body names, hash-tracked by agentic-memory) or names with `--about`, with no other test passing: 0.5. A commit passing several tests takes its highest strength. A merge's strength is the highest over its own message and every commit it brings in.

**What the weights mean.** The 0.2 merge share is a margin: a direct commit on the first-parent line is reported instead of a merge only when its strength exceeds the merge's by more than 0.25 (`0.2 / 0.8`). A direct commit naming the linked id (1.0) beats a merge matching at 0.70; a merge at 0.80 still wins. A commit inside a merge's side never competes with that merge: it folds into it, so the merge's strength is at least its own and the merge always wins.

**Only among matches.** Scores rank commits that already passed a test; the merge share never turns a non-match into a match. A round still judges every commit against every live entry before storing (20261101-112431-d-tac-9t4), and an undelivered mapping from an earlier round is replaced when a later round finds a higher-scoring commit.

**Rejected.** A 0.7/0.3 split: a 0.43 margin lets a merge matching at 0.60 beat a direct commit naming the linked id. A 0.91/0.09 split (0.10 margin): barely favors merges, though merges land most work. Ranking on raw embedder scores: their narrow band makes the merge share decide almost every case. Merge always first: a barely similar merge beats exact evidence.

**Accepted cost.** The 0.5 path strength and the 0.25 margin are judgment calls, fixed in code; tests pin each case.

**Acceptance.**
- Direct commit with linked id (1.0) vs merge at 0.70: direct reported; vs merge at 0.80: merge reported.
- A commit inside a merge never outranks that merge.
- Similarity strength is stretched above the threshold; a path-only match scores 0.5.
- Equal scores: newer commit reported.
- A later round's higher-scoring commit replaces an undelivered mapping; a lower one does not.
