Decides that agentic-memory gets no cap on the number of entries a store, shelf or seat holds, because what an agent pays for is context, which decay and the brief's folds already bound.

Storage is not the cost: the log merge folds events older than the 14-day horizon into the rows that rebuild each entry and drops the bodies of entries superseded more than 14 days ago or retired past `retired_body_lifetime` (20261025-032243-d-tac-8c2), so a store grows with its live entries, not with every change. Context is the cost, and the brief already bounds it: entries decay by kind (trap 90 days, note 14, todo 3, rule never) and notes past `brief_note_limit` fold into one line (20261026-220057-d-tac-5no). This follows the token-cost criteria of least risk first, then fewest tokens (20261029-021515-d-tac-00q): a cap saves nothing the folds do not already save, and it carries the larger risk.

Rejected: a hard cap on live entries per store or seat, evicting the coldest past it. Eviction removes entries that are cold but still true; a trap matters again the moment its trigger returns, however long it lay unused, and an evicted rule silently drops a standing instruction. Rejected too: a size cap on the log, for the same eviction reason and because the fold already keeps the log proportional to live entries.

The open edge is the brief, not the store: it prints every live rule, trap and todo whole. Where traps or todos grow until the brief outgrows its budget, the fitting answer is a per-kind brief limit beside `brief_note_limit`, with a fold line naming the command for the rest, and rules stay unlimited because each is a standing instruction. That limit is a decision of its own, taken once measurements show the growth; nothing is owed until then, so this directive is settled. `who` already reports filed against used entries per seat over 14 days, the signal that a seat files more than it uses.
