title: Merge commit evidence in landed-commit scan
summary: Directs that in the landed-commit scan a merge commit is judged together with the commits it brings in, mapping matches to the merge's sha with at most one mapping per entry per merge. It refines the landed-commit detection directive (20261101-110914-d-tac-pu1) and applies the per-entry floor at the merge's committer date per the incremental scan directive (20261101-112148-d-tac-6jm), with merge matches taking precedence over normal-commit matches.
---

In the landed-commit scan of 20261101-110914-d-tac-pu1, a merge commit is judged together with the commits it brings in: its evidence is its own message plus the full messages and changed paths of every commit reachable from its second and later parents but not from its first parent, and a match on any of them is mapped to the merge commit. Merge messages often say nothing beyond `Merge branch '<branch>'`, so the work they land is described only by the merged commits.

**Mapping.** A todo, item or trap matched through a merged commit maps to the merge commit's sha, with the subject of the merged commit that matched and the test that matched, and one merge yields at most one mapping per entry or item. A commit judged as part of a merge is not judged again on its own in the same round, so one landing reports once.

**Merge precedence.** A match through a merge commit takes precedence over a match on a normal commit, so the lookup never stops at an entry's first hit: a round judges every commit in its range against every live entry and item before it stores anything. Where an entry or item matches both a merge and normal commits, only the merge mapping is stored; where an undelivered mapping from an earlier round points at a normal commit and a later round finds a merge match for the same entry or item, the merge mapping replaces it.

**Floor.** The per-entry floor of 20261101-112148-d-tac-6jm reads the merge commit's committer date, the moment the work landed on `HEAD`, not the dates of the merged commits, which often predate the todo they settle.

**Reading it cheaply.** The merged commits lie in the round's `<mark>..HEAD` range (the mark: the last commit the previous round examined, per 20261101-112148-d-tac-6jm) whenever the merge does, so the round groups them by parent links from the stream it already read; only where a merged commit falls outside the round's batch does it read that merge's side once with `git log <first-parent>..<merge>`.

**Rejected.** Counting merged commits only when the merge message matches nothing: an informative merge message would then hide a merged commit naming a linked entry, and the rule would turn on a judgement of message quality. Mapping matches to the merged commit itself: the merged commit is not on the first-parent line, and the merge is where the work landed. Applying the floor to merged commits' own dates: work started before a todo was filed and merged after it would never match.

**Accepted cost.** Grouping commits by merge in each round; a merge whose side exceeds the batch costs one extra `git log`.

**Acceptance.**
- A merge with message `Merge branch 'x'` whose merged commit names a linked graph id, or touches an anchored path, or has a similar subject, maps the entry to the merge's sha.
- One merge matching an entry through several merged commits yields one mapping.
- A todo filed after a merged commit was authored but before the merge landed maps to the merge.
- A merged commit already judged through its merge yields no second mapping in that round.
- An entry matching a normal commit and a merge in one round stores only the merge mapping; an undelivered normal-commit mapping from an earlier round is replaced by a later merge match.
