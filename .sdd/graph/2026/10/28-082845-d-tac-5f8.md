An agentic-memory seat follows its session into the new process `claude --resume` or `claude --fork-session --resume` starts: a resume takes its parent's seat at SessionStart, and a fork takes its seat at its first prompt, because a fork's transcript does not exist yet when its SessionStart hook runs.

Why this replaces 20261027-233526-d-tac-bbp: that directive matched a fork at SessionStart by the uuid of its transcript's first record. Claude Code writes a fork's transcript only after the SessionStart hooks have run: a live `--fork-session` of a running seated session ran its `SessionStart:fork` hook at 07:23:09 and its transcript file was created at 07:23:11, so the hook read no record, matched no claim and printed the seatless notice. The first record, once written, carried exactly the parent claim's `fork_uuid`, so the matching rule holds and only its moment was wrong. Its tests and its end-to-end proof (20261027-234512-s-tac-eoz) wrote the fork's transcript before running the hook, which a real fork never does. The SessionStart input names no parent session: Claude Code's hooks reference lists `source` (`fork` among its values), `model`, `agent_type`, `session_title` and resume-cost fields, nothing pointing at the parent.

Why a seat at all: a seat is bound to its holder's claude process and start time, and a resumed or forked session runs in a new process, so it starts seatless and the notice asking it to claim is easy to skip. A forked session of 97 prompts that shipped two releases filed nothing and left its seat's state slot empty.

How a session finds its parent:

- **Resume** continues the same transcript under the same session id, so a claim whose `session_id` is the new process's session id is its parent's. This matches at SessionStart and stays there.
- **Fork** writes a new transcript under its own session id, naming no parent, and copies the parent's records from its latest compaction boundary on with their uuids unchanged, or from the first message where the parent never compacted.
- Every claim records `fork_uuid`: the uuid a fork made now would start with. The hook that writes a transcript into a claim sets it to the transcript's first record's uuid, and the first hook after a compaction replaces it with the uuid of the transcript's latest `compactMetadata` record.

When a fork is matched:

- At SessionStart a process holding no seat matches by session id as before. Where nothing matches, the source is `fork` and the transcript holds no record yet, the hook writes a marker for the session (`forks/<session id>` under the project's cache directory) and prints nothing: the seatless notice would tell the agent to claim a seat the next hook is about to give it.
- At UserPromptSubmit a process holding no seat whose session carries the marker reads its transcript's first record and matches a claim or last claim by `fork_uuid`, then removes the marker, matched or not. A match seats it as below and prints the claim's output, brief included, as the prompt hook's context; no match prints the seatless notice there, once.
- **Parent gone** (a resume, or a fork after the parent ended): the session takes the seat over, under the seats lock, as a reservation's takeover does (20261025-223640-d-tac-d2p): its process, start time, session id and transcript replace the old holder's, and the brief and replay come back as on a claim.
- **Parent alive** (a fork while the parent runs): the running holder keeps its seat (20261025-164209-d-tac-qj7). The fork gets a new seat on the parent's shelf, named as `claim --shelf` names one.
- **No match**: the seatless notice.

A session's own claim keeps working as before; the takeover only acts for a process holding no seat. A claim the agent makes before the first prompt hook wins, and the hook then removes the marker without matching.

Rejected:

- Matching any seatless session at every hook until one matches, since sessions meant to stay seatless would read their transcript head on every hook and still need a marker or a cap to stop.
- A detached waiter spawned at SessionStart that polls for the transcript and reserves the seat with `claim --session`, since it adds a background process per fork, a polling timeout and a race with the session's own hooks for the same outcome.
- Waiting inside the SessionStart hook for the transcript, since Claude Code writes the transcript after its SessionStart hooks return, so the hook would wait out its whole timeout.
- Recording only the first message uuid, searching every seated session's transcript, claiming a seat for every session, and matching by transcript path, for the reasons 20261027-233526-d-tac-bbp gave: a compacted parent's fork never carries its first uuid, the search reads megabytes per seat, auto-claims pick names for the owner, and a fork changes its path.

Accepted costs: a fork's brief arrives with its first prompt instead of at start; a fork nobody prompts never gets a seat; one marker file per seatless fork until its first prompt, swept with the cache; one read of the transcript head at that prompt.

Acceptance criteria:

1. A resumed session whose parent process is gone holds the parent's seat after SessionStart, with the brief printed, checked by a test.
2. A fork whose transcript is absent at SessionStart prints no seatless notice there and leaves a marker; at its first UserPromptSubmit, with the transcript written, it gets a new seat on a running parent's shelf, with the brief as the prompt hook's output, and the parent keeps its seat, checked by a test whose transcript appears only between the two hooks.
3. A fork of a parent that has ended takes the parent's seat at its first prompt, checked by a test.
4. A fork of a compacted session finds its parent through the boundary uuid the claim recorded after the compaction, checked by a test.
5. A fork matching no claim gets the seatless notice at its first prompt, once, and no marker remains; a startup session gets the seatless notice at SessionStart as before.
6. A fork that claims a seat itself before its first prompt keeps that seat and the marker is removed.
7. Each guard is broken once and its test watched failing; `docs/memory/` and agentic-helpers:agent-memory say a fork is seated at its first prompt in the same change.
8. Proven live on a released build: `claude --resume <id> --fork-session` beside a running seated session holds a new seat on its shelf after its first prompt.
