Token-cost choices on agentic-helpers surfaces are made by two criteria in order: first the least risky option, the one that keeps agents informed, avoids mistakes and keeps agents obeying the rules; second, among options equal on risk, the one using the fewest tokens. The owner set the criteria in these words: "what is the least risky option (the one which keeps agents informed, avoids mistakes and keeps agents obeying to rules), with the second priority what option has the least token usage".

**Surfaces covered.** Everything an agentic-helpers piece puts into an agent's context: the agentic-memory brief and per-prompt recall, hook notes, holds and refusals, MCP tool listings and replies, filing replies, and skill texts.

**How it binds.** An agent facing such a choice picks by these criteria without asking the owner, records the choice with its reasoning (as a graph decision where one results), and reports it. A choice that saves tokens by hiding information an agent acts on, or by weakening the wording of a rule agents must follow, breaks this directive. It sits beside the hidden-message rule (20261027-222117-d-tac-yjy), which keeps agent-only hook messages scarce; this one ranks the trade whenever a surface could be made cheaper.

**Why.** Every surface loads into many sessions, so a saving multiplies; but an agent left uninformed makes mistakes or drops a rule, and that costs more than the tokens saved. Ranking risk first keeps savings from eroding what the surfaces exist for.

**Rejected.** Fewest tokens as the only criterion, which trades away information agents act on. Asking the owner for each such choice, which stalls work on choices the criteria already settle.

**Accepted costs.** Some savings stay unrealized where they would raise risk. A choice taken without asking may differ from what the owner would have picked; its recorded reasoning lets it be revisited.
