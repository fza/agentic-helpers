Every skill's `SKILL.md` under `plugin/skills/` has a size budget a test enforces, and the agent-shell-guard skill splits into a core plus on-demand reference files the way agent-memory's did. Skills regrow between cuts: measured in `plugin/skills/*/SKILL.md`, agent-memory went from 16.9k to 19.3k characters after its core was cut, agent-shell-guard from 9.7k to 16.0k, agent-grounding from 12.2k to 14.1k, agent-capture from 10.8k to 13.9k. A skill loads whole into context each time it is needed, so its growth is paid on every load. The insight 20261031-122251-s-tac-jgs found splitting whole-context loads into cores the largest win, and the criteria 20261029-021515-d-tac-00q weigh least risk first: a reference file is read only when its trigger shows up.

**Rule.** A test in `source/hooks` reads each `plugin/skills/*/SKILL.md` and fails where one exceeds its budget, recorded beside the test with the reason for each figure. Raising a budget is a change of its own, stating what the added text earns. The agent-shell-guard core keeps what an agent needs at a refusal (the reasons and their fixes); what a reason line points to moves to reference files named from the core's "Read when" table.

**Rejected.** Cutting again with no budget: the skills regrew after the last cut within days. A budget for the whole skill directory: one file's growth would hide behind another's cut.

**Accepted cost.** Every skill change fits its budget or raises it on purpose.

**Acceptance.**
- The budget test fails when a `SKILL.md` is padded past its budget, and passes on the tree.
- agent-shell-guard's `SKILL.md` falls under its budget with no reason line or fix lost; its reference files carry the rest.
- `docs/plugin/architecture.md` names the budgets and where they live.
