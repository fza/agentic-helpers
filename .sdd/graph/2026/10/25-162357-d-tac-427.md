An entry can be shared: it reaches seats besides the one that filed it, and each receiving seat treats it as its own without any flag, and hears when it arrives.

**Who receives an entry.** A seat receives an entry when any of these holds, as a plain union:

- the entry names the seat: `agentic-memory new ... --for <seat>`, repeatable, MCP key `for` (an array of seat names), stored in the entry's `new` event as seat ids;
- the entry is for every seat: `--for all`, stored as `"for": "all"`, reaching every seat present and future; `all` becomes a reserved word no seat can be named;
- the seat follows one of the entry's topics: `agentic-memory follow <topic>...` and `unfollow <topic>...` (MCP `follow`, `unfollow`, key `topic`) change the follows of the seat the calling session holds, stored in that seat's one `seats.json` entry as `{"name": "idea", "follows": ["infra"]}`. Follows resolve through topic merges like every other topic, and a `tag` adding a followed topic makes the entry reach the follower from then on.

The filing seat is never its own recipient. An entry line names its recipients after the filer: `[main → all, ...]`, `[main → idea, review, ...]`, `[main → infra followers, ...]` where only a follow brings it.

**What receiving means.** A received entry stands with the seat's own: in its brief by heat, in `search` without `--seat` or `--all-seats`, and in prompt recall at the own seat's floor (two standard deviations above the mean, not three). Ownership does not move: `retire`, `retain`, `retitle` and `tag` stay the filer's; `supersede` and `confirm` stay open to every seat, as now.

**Hearing about it.** Each seat keeps a seen mark, `briefs/<seat-id>.seen.json` in the cache, holding the time of the newest received entry it was told about. Three channels tell it about every received entry filed after the mark, as the lines `agentic-memory: new for <seat> (<n>):` and one entry line each, then move the mark:

1. **The next prompt**: the `UserPromptSubmit` hook adds them as hidden context beside recall.
2. **The next tool call**: a `PostToolUse` hook on every tool adds them as `additionalContext`. It compares the log's tail size and manifest against the mark first and folds the log only when they moved, so a call with nothing new costs two `stat` calls.
3. **An idle session**: a `Stop` hook marked `asyncRewake` in `plugin/hooks/hooks.json` starts a waiter when a seated session's turn ends. It checks the log every 5 seconds, exits 0 after 30 minutes with nothing new, and on an arrival exits 2 with the lines, which Claude Code hands to the session as a new turn. Each turn end arms a new waiter; a session holding no seat starts none.

Why: work split across seats needs a way for one seat to hand another a finding, a blocker or a changed fact without the receiver having to know to search for it. Naming seats targets a handover; following a topic lets a seat take in a subject's news without every filer knowing who cares. Both answer "does this seat receive it" with yes or no, so they combine as a union. An entry nobody is told about waits until someone searches; the prompt and tool-call notes reach a working session within one call, and the waiter reaches one waiting at its prompt, which is how fdbox's autopilot already wakes its jobs.

Rejected: a `--shared` switch reaching every seat (cannot address a subset); topic subscriptions alone (the filer cannot hand something to one seat); waking only seats that opt in (a per-seat switch to remember, and a handover to an interactive seat would sit until someone typed there); the receiver searching with `--all-seats` (it has to know something arrived first).

Accepted costs: one waiter process per idle seated session, polling every 5 seconds for up to 30 minutes; a woken session starts a turn without its user typing, spending tokens on it; a `PostToolUse` hook now runs on every tool call; `all` can no longer name a seat; the hidden notes add context to every prompt and call that has news.

Acceptance criteria:

1. `new --for idea --for review` stores both seat ids; `--for all` stores `all`; a seat named `all` is refused at claim and rename.
2. `follow infra` as seat `idea` puts `infra` in idea's `seats.json` entry; an entry filed under `infra` by `main` reaches `idea`; after `merge infra infrastructure` it still does; `unfollow infra` stops it.
3. A received entry appears in the receiver's brief, its default-scope search, and its prompt recall at the own floor; a test asserts each, and that the filer is not a recipient.
4. The receiver's `retire` of a received entry is refused naming its owner; its `supersede` lands.
5. With an entry for `idea` filed after idea's seen mark, idea's next prompt and next tool call each carry `agentic-memory: new for idea (1):` once, and the mark moves; a tool call with no log change does not fold the log.
6. The `Stop` waiter exits 2 with the lines within 10 seconds of an arrival, exits 0 after its deadline with none, and is not started for a seatless session; the poll interval and deadline are injectable for the test.
7. `plugin/hooks/hooks.json` wires the `PostToolUse` hook and the `asyncRewake` `Stop` hook; `claude plugin validate .` passes.
8. `README.md` and `agentic-helpers:agent-memory` describe `--for`, `follow`, `unfollow`, what receiving means, and the three channels.
