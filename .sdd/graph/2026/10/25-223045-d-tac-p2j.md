agentic-memory seats are durable or temporary. A temporary seat keeps its entries only while it is in use: once its holder closes, a retention period runs, and when it runs out the seat and the entries it filed are purged from the store. Claiming the seat again before then keeps everything. A seat is durable unless set otherwise, as every seat is today.

Why: a seat for a one-off job (a review, a spike, a scratch session) otherwise leaves its entries in every later brief and search until each is retired by hand.

## What

- **One subcommand sets it: `agentic-memory lifetime durable | temporary | <duration>`**, on the seat the session holds. `temporary` makes the seat temporary with the default retention, and is how a set retention is unset; a duration makes it temporary with that retention; `durable` makes it durable, and the same command makes a durable seat temporary again. Durations take `m`, `h` or `d` (`90m`, `12h`, `2d`); `0` purges at close. `claim <seat> --lifetime durable|temporary|<duration>` sets it while claiming. MCP: tool `lifetime` with key `value`, and key `lifetime` on `claim`.
- **The default retention is the plugin option `temporary_seat_lifetime`**, a string in the syntax `lifetime` takes; empty means `1d`. A value that does not parse falls back to `1d` with a note. One duration format serves the command and the option.
- **The setting lives on the seat's `seats.json` entry**, beside `name` and `follows`, so it survives a close and a re-claim. The close time goes on the claim the seat keeps as `<seat>.last.json`.
- **A seat closes when its holder is gone**: the holding process no longer passes `seat.Alive`, or the holder runs `release`. A release counts because a released seat has no holder left to start the clock later. SessionEnd with reason `clear` or `resume` is no close: the process lives on and keeps its seat.
- **The clock starts at the close**: at SessionEnd with any other reason, or at `release`. A process that ended without SessionEnd (a crash, a kill) closed at its transcript's last write, its last sign of life.
- **Eviction purges.** When retention runs out, a log merge drops every row of every entry the seat filed, bodies included, and the seat leaves the live seats of `seats.json`, so its name frees. Nothing of a purged entry is left to search, list or revive. The merge policy decides it; no event kind is added, so a binary of an earlier release still reads the merged segment.
- **Retention `0` purges inside SessionEnd or `release`**, in the seat's own store.
- **Every SessionStart, in any project, checks every store** under `~/.claude/agentic-memory/` and purges the temporary seats whose retention has run out. A store with nothing expired is only read; the lock and the merge run only where something expired.
- **Entries the seat filed with `--for` survive the purge.** Every other entry it filed goes, entries that reached followers of their topics included. With the filer gone, every seat the entry names (every seat, for `--for all`) may `retire`, `retain`, `retitle` and `tag` it. A line shows the filer as `<name> (expired)`: the registry keeps an expired seat's id and name apart from the live seats, so the name is free for a new seat, and drops them once no surviving entry names the seat.
- **Entries other seats filed for the temporary seat stay**: they are their filers'. The purged seat drops out of their recipients.

## Rejected

- **Retiring every entry and dropping bodies at once.** Titles and topics would stay in retired listings forever and the seat stay registered: a seat meant to leave nothing behind would leave its index behind.
- **Retiring every entry with the usual `retired_body_days` keep.** Bodies would stay readable 90 days and revivable 14: ordinary retirement, not eviction.
- **The clock starting when a later run notices a crashed holder.** Retention would stretch by however long nobody looked.
- **Purging only from the seat's own project.** A project left alone would keep an expired seat's entries on disk for as long as nobody opens it, which is what a temporary seat promises not to do.
- **A scheduled OS job** (`launchd`, `cron`) sweeping every store. Evicts on time with no session running, but the plugin would install and own a per-platform scheduler entry, a surface it has nowhere else.
- **Two verbs, `temporary [<duration>]` and `durable`.** Plainer to read, but two subcommands for one setting.
- **Flags on `claim` only, a re-claim of one's own seat changing it.** `claim`, refused today on a seat the session holds, would gain a second meaning.
- **The word retention in the command.** `retain <id>... <level>` already sets an entry's decay level; one word for two unrelated clocks blurs both.
- **A number option, `temporary_seat_hours` or `temporary_seat_days`**, like `retired_body_days`. The command would take `2d` while the option took `48` or `2`: two formats for one quantity, and days need fractions below a day.
- **Purging `--for` entries with the rest.** A handover or blocker a temporary seat filed for another seat would vanish from that seat's brief at expiry.
- **Keeping `--for` entries until every recipient has seen them.** `--for all` names future seats and never completes, and the purge would wait on other seats' activity.
- **Counting topic-follow deliveries as shared.** Nearly every entry reaches some follower, so almost nothing would be purged.

## Accepted costs

- Links into a purged entry, from another seat's entries, read `gone`.
- The merge policy drops whole rows, where until now it dropped only bodies.
- Every session start reads every project's store on the machine.
- With no session started anywhere, an expired seat stays on disk until one is.
- A surviving `--for` entry has no single owner: the ownership rule gains an exception for an expired filer.

## Acceptance criteria

- `lifetime temporary`, `lifetime <duration>` and `lifetime durable` change the held seat's `seats.json` entry; `lifetime` with no seat held, or with a value that is neither word nor duration, is refused and changes nothing. `claim --lifetime` does the same on the claimed seat.
- A temporary seat whose holder ended through SessionEnd (reason not `clear`/`resume`) or `release` records the close time; SessionEnd with `clear` or `resume` records none and the seat stays held.
- A holder gone without SessionEnd is closed at its transcript's last write.
- Claiming a closed temporary seat before its retention runs out keeps every entry and clears the close time.
- Once retention has run out, a SessionStart in another project purges the seat: its non-`--for` entries are absent from `search`, `search --status retired|superseded`, `show` and the brief, `revive` names an unknown id, and its name can be claimed as a new seat.
- Retention `0` purges at SessionEnd or `release`.
- A `--for` entry of a purged seat stays in its recipients' brief and search, shows its filer as `<name> (expired)`, and a recipient seat may retire, retain, retitle and tag it; a seat it does not name may not.
- An entry another seat filed `--for` the temporary seat survives the purge unchanged for its filer.
- `temporary_seat_lifetime` empty or unparsable means `1d`, the unparsable value with a note.
- Every new guard is broken and watched failing on its own test, the file restored byte-identically.
- `docs/memory/` and the `agent-memory` skill describe `lifetime`, temporary seats and `temporary_seat_lifetime` in the same change.
