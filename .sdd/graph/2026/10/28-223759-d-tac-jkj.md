An agentic-memory todo can hold one todolist: items recorded beside its body, each with a stable id, ticked, unticked, dropped, removed, added and re-sorted through `ack`, with linked items ticking themselves when the entry they name ends.

Why: across every live store on one machine (1067 entries, 126 todos), a quarter of todos are written as lists, 32 of them with about six items each. A body never changes, so ticking one item meant superseding the whole todo: one list was rewritten nine times, growing from 218 to 574 words, another seven times, and superseded todo bodies hold 7264 words written out again. Most rewrites reworded items they did not change, so an item could drift or drop out unnoticed. Links (20261025-180343-d-tac-p9f) already state what became of each target, and end conditions (20261026-215617-d-tac-45q) already retire a todo on a watched handle; what was missing is a per-item state that costs a few tokens instead of a rewrite. The measurement and the exploration behind this are in [the record](./28-223759-d-tac-jkj/todolist-measurement-and-exploration.md).

Items:

- One todolist per entry, on the `todo` kind (20261026-215042-d-tac-g5a) only.
- An item is a record in the store's log, a new memorylog event kind `item` (add, check, uncheck, drop, remove, order), never a line of the body. The body stays prose.
- Each item gets an id of three lowercase base36 characters, drawn at random, unique within its todo and never reused. `<todo>#<id>`, for example `m-abc#k3x`, is the only address of an item.
- An item is plain text, or a memory id or full graph id (a qualified foreign id included); a linked item shows its target's title.
- `new --kind todo --item '<text>'` or `--item <id>`, repeatable (MCP `item`, a list), files the todolist with the todo. `--item` on another kind is refused.

Changing items, all through `ack` (20261026-220743-d-tac-8lf), whose MCP tool gains `undo`, `remove`, `add` and `order`:

- `ack <todo>#<id>... --done` ticks; `--undo` unticks.
- `--decline '<why>'` drops: decided not to do it. A dropped item stays listed with its reason and counts toward done.
- `--remove` removes: the item never belonged (a typo, the wrong list, a duplicate). It leaves the count, the brief and `show`; `show --with-removed` lists it.
- `ack <todo> --add '<text>'` or `--add <id>`, repeatable, adds items at the end.
- `ack <todo> --order <id>,<id>...` puts the named items first in that order; every other item keeps its relative order behind them.
- `ack <rule id> --done` or `--decline` keeps settling this session's do-now rules. An item flag on a rule, a do-now settlement on an item, and a position number as an address are refused.

Linked items:

- A linked item's state is derived at read, never stored: a graph target closed or a memory target retired ticks it, and a target that reopens unticks it. An explicit `--done`, `--decline` or `--remove` wins over the derived state.
- A superseded target does not tick it. The item follows the supersede chain to the newest entry and ticks when that entry closes or retires, shown `→ <target> → <newest> <state>`. A target that is gone keeps the item open, flagged `gone`.
- `--until closed:` and `retire?` keep reading superseded as ended, as 20261026-215617-d-tac-45q set: a todo waiting on a decision is moot once the decision is replaced, while an item naming work still owes it when the work moves elsewhere.

Display:

- An item line reads `<n>. #<id>: <text>`, and a linked item adds `  → <target id> <state>` in the link-flag style. `<n>` is the item's position in the current order, for display only. Every field has a fixed shape, so a line splits without guessing.
- In the text brief, the one the hooks give the agent at claim, session start and compaction, a todo's short line (20261026-220057-d-tac-5no) gains `<done>/<total>`, followed by up to three open items in order on indented lines, each text cut at about 60 characters. Ticked, dropped and removed items are hidden, and the links of items show only through the count.
- `show` lists every item but removed ones: `<n>. [ ] #<id>: <text>`, `[x]` ticked, `[-]` dropped with `  dropped: <why>`.
- `brief --json`, `show --json` and the MCP reads carry `items` on the todo object, each with `id`, `position`, `text`, `link`, `state` (open, done, dropped) and `reason`, beside `done` and `total`.

End: a todo whose every item is ticked or dropped has reached a declared end. The end check retires it with `end reached: items <n>/<n>`, lists it once under `ended since last brief`, and `revive` undoes it. With `--until` conditions as well, whichever comes first ends it. A linked target reopening afterwards does not bring the todo back.

Supersede: superseding a todo that holds a todolist requires `--items carry|leave` (MCP `items`) and is refused without it. `carry` moves the whole list to the successor, open, ticked and dropped items alike, with ids and order kept; a successor's own item whose id collides gets a new id, named in the reply, and `<old>#<id>` resolves through the supersede chain. `leave` keeps the list on the superseded todo.

Lists written into a body: a todo whose body holds a run of list lines (`- `, `1. `, `- [ ] `) lands as written, and the reply says `<n> list lines in the text; steps go into --item`. Not every list is a todolist, a list of settled points among open ones for instance, so the tool never turns lines into items and existing todos are not migrated. agentic-helpers:agent-memory says when to use a todolist (the steps of one piece of work, never a supersede to tick a step), the `item` field of the `new` tool carries a one-line description read when the tool's schema loads, and the reply catches a list filed into the body anyway; the three change together.

Store format: `item` is appended to memorylog's event kinds, and the memory store format moves up one, migrated under the store lock with a backup as earlier formats were. Binaries before the release cannot read the store.

Rejected alternatives:

- Items as `- [ ]` lines of the body, with only ticks as records: adding or rewording an item still supersedes the whole todo, and most of the measured growth came from added items.
- Every item a memory entry of its own: about 100 tokens or more per add with the filing reply, every item in search and recall, decaying alone, embedded alone, and held to a title of 3 to 10 words.
- Turning a body's list lines into items at filing: misfiles a list that is not a todolist. Refusing such a todo: a retry for every prose list, with refusal wording to maintain.
- Ordinal numbers as item identity: a re-sort renumbers items already quoted in a state slot or another entry. A linked item addressed by its target id: two address forms, and a graph id of about 30 characters to copy correctly after a compaction.
- A new `item` command and MCP tool: one more tool in every session's context. `retire` and `revive` on `<todo>#<id>`: done and dropped look alike, retired already means an entry ended, and nothing adds.
- A superseded target ticking its item: ticks work that only moved. Changing `closed:` the same way: replaces part of 20261026-215617-d-tac-45q, and todos waiting on a replaced decision stay open.
- Listing a finished todo under `retire?` only, or ending it only under an opt-in `--until items`: retiring stays the agent's chore, the failure end conditions answered.
- Move-one-item flags (`--first`, `--before`): a call per moved item. Priority levels: no order within a level, and one more field to keep current.
- Other brief layouts: the count alone costs a `show` after every compaction; every open item costs about 60 tokens per list per brief; one line with ` | ` separators runs near 250 characters; JSON in the hook brief repeats keys on every entry (one seat's JSON brief is 21.8 KB); fields without separators, as in `2 #p9a m-fqzb1k Size limits`, cannot be split.
- Ticks through the state slot, tags, commit trailers, or a flush at compaction: the slot is overwritten prose, tags are topic labels, a trailer is bound to a branch and a commit, and the compaction nudge does not fire reliably.

Accepted costs:

- A store format bump.
- `ack` carries two meanings, settling this session's do-now rules and changing a todo's items for good; its description and the skill explain both.
- Superseded means ended for `--until closed:` and moved for items.
- Up to three lines, about 45 tokens, per todolist in every brief.
- A retire nobody asked for when a list finishes.
- The brief's next items are only as true as the agent's ticks.
- The reply wording on list lines becomes part of the tool's contract, quoted by the skill.

Acceptance criteria:

1. `new --kind todo --item` files items with distinct ids; `--item` on another kind is refused; MCP the same.
2. A table-driven fold test covers `--done`, `--undo`, `--decline`, `--remove`, `--add` and `--order`; an unknown item address, a position number as an address, an item flag on a rule and a do-now settlement on an item are refused.
3. A linked item ticks when its graph target closes or its memory target retires, unticks when the target reopens, follows a supersede chain to its newest entry, and stays open flagged `gone` for a missing target, each with a faked `sdd export`.
4. The brief prints `<done>/<total>` and up to three open items in order in the fixed layout; `show` lists every item but removed ones; JSON carries `items`.
5. A todo with every item ticked or dropped retires with `end reached: items <n>/<n>`, appears once under `ended since last brief`, and `revive` restores it.
6. Supersede of a todo holding a todolist without `--items` is refused; `carry` moves the list with ids and order kept and gives a colliding id a new one; `leave` keeps the list behind.
7. A todo body with list lines lands, and the reply names `--item`.
8. A store of the previous format migrates under lock with a backup, and an older binary cannot read the migrated store.
9. agentic-helpers:agent-memory, `docs/memory/usage.md` and `docs/memory/architecture.md` name todolists, every `ack` flag, the brief and `show` layouts, the end and the reply wording, in the same change.
