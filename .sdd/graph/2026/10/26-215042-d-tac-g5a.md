Every agentic-memory entry has a kind that decides how fast it decays and where the brief shows it, and each seat gets one state slot for where its work stands in place of progress entries. `agentic-memory new` takes `--kind rule|trap|todo|note` (MCP `kind`) in place of `--retention`. Where a seat's work stands is no longer an entry: each seat holds one state slot, overwritten by `agentic-memory state` (MCP `state`, body on stdin), and printed first in the brief with its age.

Why: retention, the level the agentic-memory directive (20261025-014326-d-tac-mvn) has the filer pick, was a guess made once, and the guess was wrong most of the time. A heavy seat's memory over two days was about 35% progress snapshots stale within the hour, 25% owner decisions waiting for capture, 20% to-dos for one later task filed with long retention, and 5% traps, the kind that stays useful longest. Retention only sets how fast heat decays (`cache.go:96`), so every one of them stayed in the brief until someone retired it by hand, and the traps and the rules sat among the snapshots. What an entry is says how long it matters and where it belongs; the filer knows that at filing time, where it cannot know a lifetime.

The kinds:

- `rule`: a standing grant or instruction. Never decays, printed in the standing section. The tool refuses it unless `--from user`, closing the gap where `--retention standing` accepts `--from agent` and `--from tool` (`entries.go:49`) and the user-only rule lived in the skill alone.
- `trap`: something that already cost time and will again. Decays at today's `long` rate, printed in its own section above todos and notes.
- `todo`: work owed later, including an owner decision waiting for capture into the graph. Decays at today's `short` rate. What ends it is decided apart, with expiry tied to an event.
- `note`: everything else, the catch-all. Decays at today's `normal` rate.

The state slot:

- `agentic-memory state` overwrites the calling seat's slot. The slot keeps body, time, session, branch and commit, written as a `state` row in the log, never an entry: it holds no id, no heat, no topics, and is outside `search`, recall, retire and the live and retired counts.
- The brief opens with it, after its header line: `state (<age> old, branch <b>): <body>`, the age measured at brief time.
- A state write moves the replay anchor as an entry does (`newestOwn`, `seats.go:306`). Without that the replay since the newest entry would grow past `maxPieces` once snapshots stop being filed, the replay nudge would fire constantly, and agents would file snapshot entries again to quiet it.
- A state write answers the compaction nudge and re-arms the replay nudge (20261025-192326-d-tac-303), as any entry does.
- The compaction nudge and the replay nudge ask for a state write first and an entry only for a finding, an open question or a judgment call. Their wording changes in the same change as agentic-helpers:agent-memory, which quotes them.
- The log's horizon fold keeps each seat's newest `state` row and drops older ones.
- The word limit (`entry_max_words`) applies to the state body.
- `brief --json` (20261026-214732-d-tac-y5y) carries it as `state` (`body`, `at`, `branch`, `commit`), and its entry lists split by kind: `rules`, `traps`, `todos`, `notes`.

Brief order: header, state, changes by other seats, rules, traps, todos, notes, new from other seats, topics. The cap that keeps standing entries to a third of the brief's 9000-character budget (`brief.go`) applies to rules.

Changing a kind: `agentic-memory rekind <id> <kind>` (MCP `rekind`), allowed to every seat on the entry's shelf, like `retitle`. Rekinding to `rule` is refused unless the entry was filed `--from user`. `retain <id>... <level>` stays as an override of the decay a kind sets, with its levels unchanged.

Topics stay free labels for subjects, at least one per entry, unchanged. A topic no longer carries lifecycle: no `state` or `capture-owed` topic is needed.

Migration: the store format moves up one, migrated by the first call of the new binary under the store lock with a backup under `backup/`, as the shelf storage format (20261025-232347-d-tac-5rh) and earlier formats did. Every `standing` entry becomes `rule` and keeps its `from`; every other entry becomes `note` and keeps its retention as a `retain` override, so no entry moves in anyone's brief at migration. Binaries before the release cannot read the migrated store.

Rejected alternatives:

- A kind beside retention, the kind setting a default that `--retention` overrides: two knobs for one question, so filers keep guessing retention and a to-do filed `long` stays possible.
- No kinds, only the state slot: rules and traps keep competing with notes in one list ordered by heat, and a do-on-claim rule stays prose among the standing entries.
- `state` and `hand-over` as kinds: a state entry that supersedes its predecessor still costs an id, a log entry and a line wherever superseded entries show; a slot costs none. Hand-over already exists as `--for`.
- State writes leaving the replay anchor where it is: the replay would outgrow its pieces as soon as snapshots stop, as above.
- A state slot per shelf: shelf mates do separate work, and one slot would let each overwrite the other's.

Accepted costs:

- A breaking CLI and MCP change: `--retention` and the MCP `retention` key on `new` go, and a call passing them is refused naming `--kind`.
- A store format bump that older binaries cannot read.
- agentic-helpers:agent-memory, `docs/memory/usage.md` and `docs/memory/architecture.md` rewritten for kinds and the slot.
- A one-line state can stand in for turns the replay would otherwise have carried. The same holds for a one-line entry today.

Acceptance criteria:

1. `new --kind rule|trap|todo|note` files with the kind's decay; `new --retention` is refused naming `--kind`; MCP the same.
2. `new --kind rule` without `--from user` is refused; `rekind <id> rule` on an entry not filed `--from user` is refused.
3. `state` overwrites the seat's slot; the brief opens with it and its age; a second write replaces the first; the slot never appears in `search`, recall or the live count.
4. A state write moves the replay anchor, answers the compaction nudge and re-arms the replay nudge, each checked by a test.
5. The brief lists sections in the order named above, checked by a test.
6. A format migration test: a store of the previous format migrates under lock, standing entries read as `rule`, others as `note` with their retention kept as an override, and a backup exists.
7. The horizon fold keeps only each seat's newest `state` row.
8. Skill and docs name the kinds, the slot, `rekind`, and the new nudge wording, in the same change.
