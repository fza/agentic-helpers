title: Per-request embedding timeout with fill lock
summary: agentic-memory and agentic-tools bound each embedding request by `embedding_timeout` instead of the whole fill, and guard fills with a non-blocking `fill.lock` so concurrent processes skip filling and search existing vectors, mirroring sdd's index pipeline. It keeps the per-request saving of 20261031-023144-d-tac-vcz and replaces its fill-wide deadline, and aligns `embedding_timeout` with sdd's per-request `embedding.timeout` (20261030-083307-d-tac-q74). ---

agentic-memory and agentic-tools fill their vectors the way sdd fills its search index: `embedding_timeout` bounds each embedding request rather than the whole fill, and a fill runs under a non-blocking lock, so a process finding another one filling the same vector store skips its fill and searches the vectors held so far.

**sdd's design.** At e64cff82, `pkg/llm/embed/decorate.go:30` puts `embedding.timeout` on each Embed call and `internal/handlers/index_pipeline.go:103-133` embeds one batch per call, publishing its finished entries before the next, with no deadline over the run. `internal/index/store.go:189-211` takes the fill lock; a search's lazy fill (`internal/cliapp/search.go:541-552`) takes it without waiting and, when another process holds it, searches the index as it stands. Entries and queries are embedded the same way in both tools, so one design serves both.

**What changes.** The saving per request of 20261031-023144-d-tac-vcz stays. `fill` in `source/hooks/internal/memory/vectors.go` and the rebuild in `source/hooks/internal/tools/vectors.go` give each request its own `embedding_timeout` deadline; a caller's own deadline, such as the 5s of a prompt's recall, still bounds the whole call. A file `fill.lock` in each vector store's directory, taken with `filelock.TryExclusive`, guards the fill: held by another process, the fill embeds nothing and the search, recall or filing scores against the rows already held.

**Why.** A deadline over the whole fill caps how much of a re-embed one call can land, so a large store or a busy embedder needs many calls, each loading the embedder for the share it cannot finish; per request, one search lands the whole re-embed as sdd's does. Without a lock, every session's prompt hooks and searches embed the same missing texts at once against one embedder.

**Rejected.**
- Keeping the fill-wide deadline: one call lands only what fits in it.
- A blocking lock: a prompt's recall would wait on another session's re-embed.

**Accepted costs.** A search after a fingerprint change takes as long as the re-embed; while another process fills, results cover only the entries embedded so far.

**Acceptance.**
1. A fill whose requests each fit `embedding_timeout` completes however many requests it needs.
2. A fill finding `fill.lock` held embeds nothing and the search still answers from the rows held.
3. Both memory and the tool library behave so.
4. Tests cover each, and each new guard is broken once to watch its test fail.
