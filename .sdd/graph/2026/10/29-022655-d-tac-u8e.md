The agentic-memory brief and per-prompt recall fold ended link flags into a count, and the brief flags `retire?` candidates on the line already printed instead of repeating it; recall changes in no other way. Both print entries through `shortLine` in `source/hooks/internal/memory/render.go`, the short line of 20261026-220057-d-tac-5no.

**Link flags on the short line.**
- Listed in full: links to open targets and to settled graph targets (a decision in force; the same reading `inferredEnd` in `ends.go` gives, where settled is not ended).
- Folded into one count by how they ended: closed, retired, gone. A superseded target keeps its successor id, for example `→ 17 ended, 1 superseded by <id>; ← 1`.
- `← n` is unchanged. `show`, `search` and every full line keep the per-link list.

**`retire?` in place.** A brief entry that looks ended gets a `retire? <reason>` flag on its line under rules, traps, todos or notes. The `retire?` section of 20261026-215617-d-tac-45q keeps only candidates printed nowhere above (notes past `brief_note_limit`), each as `<id> <title>  retire? <reason>`, title kept, flags dropped. No line prints twice.

**Recall keeps its form.** Per-prompt recall (`recall` in `hooks.go`) changes only through the link fold it shares via `shortLine`.

The choices follow the token-cost criteria of 20261029-021515-d-tac-00q: least risk to what agents know and obey first, fewest tokens second.

Why: link flags were 1.9k of a 7.2k-character brief, one note with 18 graph links taking 1.2k; ended links need no action, the successor id is what an agent follows, and a settled decision is context in force. The `retire?` section repeated whole lines of entries already shown, about 600 characters with four candidates. Recall measured over three days: 1976 blocks, 412k characters, about 208 (about 50 tokens) per prompt; hit lines about 74%, link flags 12%, cited-by sublines 14%; 16% of hits named in one of the next 15 tool calls, a lower bound since use is mostly silent. Its selection is relative only (mean + k deviations), with no absolute floor.

Rejected:
- Links as a count only (hides successor ids); a cap at three flags (still about 300 characters, may hide open links); folding settled links too (the brief and `retire?` would disagree on settled, and the decision in force would need a `show`).
- `retire?` as ids only (an unlisted candidate shows no title, so judging it takes a `show`, less informed than before).
- Recall cited-by sublines without `[seat, date]` (about 5% saved, but the agent loses which seat filed the follow-up and how recent it is); an absolute similarity floor for recall (removes weak hits on short prompts, but a floor set too high hides a needed entry, and silent use makes that risk unmeasurable).

Accepted costs: who closed a link takes one `show`; many settled links still print each; candidates spread through the brief, found by their flag; about 45 tokens per prompt remain in recall, weak hits on short prompts included.

Acceptance:
- A brief line of an entry with ended, settled, open and superseded links prints open and settled in full and folds the rest with successor ids; `show` and `search` are unchanged; a test fails when the fold is removed.
- A test brief with a listed and an over-cap candidate flags the listed one in place and leaves it out of the section; the over-cap one appears in the section with title and reason.
- The `agentic-helpers:agent-memory` skill lines naming brief flags and `retire?` change with it.
