An agentic-memory todo or note may carry one due time, at which the entry rises in front of the agent: before it nears, the brief spends no line on it beyond a count; once it nears or passes, it stands in a capped due section of its own, and a running session gets one line when it comes due. The due time is for the agent, never shown to the owner, and serves ranking only: it wakes nothing on its own and adds no other field.

Why: agents note work that matters only from a certain moment (check a migration tomorrow, rerun a measurement next week). Written into a title, such a time ranks like any other entry, so the entry costs a brief line at every claim and compaction until then and, as a todo decaying at its short rate, may have sunk by the time it matters. With the time known to the tool, the entry stays out of the way until it is near and comes up when it is due.

Filing and changing:

- `agentic-memory new --due <time>` (MCP `new` key `due`), on a `todo` or a `note` only; on a `rule` or `trap` it is refused with `--due takes a todo or a note`. The time takes the `at:` forms of the end conditions (20261026-215617-d-tac-45q): RFC 3339, or a date meaning its start in local time.
- `agentic-memory due <id> <time>` moves it, `agentic-memory due <id> --clear` drops it (MCP tool `due`); any seat on the entry's shelf (the memory the agent sessions sharing it read and write) may, as with `retitle` and `tag`.
- `show`, `search` and the JSON entry object carry it as `due`.

The brief:

- A due section right after the state, before rules: `due (<shown> of <total>):`, ordered by due time, each short line ending `due 2h ago` or `due in 5h`.
- It holds entries already due and those due within one day. They leave their kind section, never listed twice.
- Its own cap of 5 entries, then `+<n> more due: agentic-memory brief --due`. The kind sections (20261026-220057-d-tac-5no) keep their caps and their content, so due entries never push out another entry.
- An entry due later than one day is out of its kind section, replaced by one line `<n> due later (next <time>)`. Not hidden: search, recall, `show`, `view` and `brief --due` list it.
- `brief --due` (MCP `brief` key `due`) lists every live entry carrying a due time, ordered by it.

In a running session: when an entry on the seat's shelf comes due, the next prompt, tool call or idle wake prints `agentic-memory: due for <seat> (<n>):` and the short lines, with the same cap of 5 and footer. Once per entry per seat. The idle waiter's 30-minute window is accepted; an entry coming due while no session runs shows in the next brief.

Leaving: an entry stops being due when retired or moved. One still live 7 days past its due time is flagged `retire?` as the end conditions' inferred ends are (20261026-215617-d-tac-45q).

Unchanged: heat (20261026-222129-d-tac-ak9) gets no due boost, so search, recall and `view` rank as before; `view` gains one filter word, `due`, selecting entries that carry a due time. The choices follow the token-cost criteria (20261029-021515-d-tac-00q): a far-off entry costs one count line instead of a line per brief, and nothing an agent acts on is hidden.

Rejected alternatives:

- A due boost folded into heat: heat is cached on disk and ranks search, recall and `view`, which stay as they are, and a share of one score cannot be capped.
- Due entries competing inside their kind sections: a due note would push out the hottest notes of the last session, and a due todo prints anyway, so it gains nothing.
- An exact wake at the due time: hooks run only on events, and re-arming the idle waiter every 30 minutes spends a turn each time.
- `--when at:<time>`: `--when` means firing on a tool call for traps; ranking from a time is a different rule behind the same word.
- A due time on rules and traps: a rule tied to a moment has `--on`, and a trap fires on a path, command or tool.
- A due time per todolist item: a second place to store and show one, against keeping metadata scarce.
- Writing the time into the title: works without the tool, but the entry ranks and costs as any other until then.

Accepted costs:

- A new flag, command, MCP tool and key, view word and brief section.
- An entry due within the day takes one of the 5 due places before its time.
- One hidden line per entry per seat when it comes due mid-session.

Acceptance criteria:

1. `new --due` takes RFC 3339 and a date; a malformed time is refused naming the forms; `--due` on a rule or trap is refused; MCP the same.
2. `due <id> <time>` moves and `due <id> --clear` drops it, for any seat on the shelf, refused for another shelf's entry; MCP the same.
3. A table-driven brief test: entries due long ago, within the day and later than a day sit in the due section, the due section and the `due later` line respectively, never also in their kind section, with the cap of 5 and its footer.
4. With more due entries than the cap, every kind section prints as it does without them.
5. A tool call after an entry comes due prints the due line once per entry and seat, and the waiter wakes an idle session on it.
6. An entry 7 days past due is flagged `retire?`; heat, search and recall order are unchanged by `due`.
7. `brief --due`, `view` with `due`, `show`, `search` and the JSON entry object carry the due time.
8. Skill and docs name the flag, command, sections, line wordings and the 7-day flag in the same change.
