shell-guard gives a Bash call whose command name is not fixed text the outside-project verdict, in agentic-helpers commits 5ca11a7, c84c668 and 88ce142, not yet released, completing 20261027-090426-d-tac-gj8. The ask-twice-refuse rule and no-argument names that can split are a separate directive, 20261027-101534-d-tac-jlu, still open.

Each criterion with its test in `internal/shellguard/`:

1. `$'\162m' -rf /`, `$CMD -rf /` and `"$tool" build` get the verdict with the reason `cannot judge: the command name <word> is not fixed text` under ask, deny and off; `$CMD` alone and `rm -rf ./build` keep their verdicts: TestACommandNameThatIsNotFixedTextTakesTheOutsideVerdict, also over `$(which rm)`, a name after `ls &&`, behind an assignment, inside `bash -c`, behind `sudo` and through `find -exec`.
2. The ask follows the permission mode: TestAnAskFollowsThePermissionMode and a countdown case in TestACountdownDeniesAnAskNobodyAnswers.
3. Skill and docs name the reason: TestTheSkillQuotesEveryRefusal.

Beyond the directive: a launcher option or assignment whose value may split into several words (`env FOO=$x make`, `timeout $secs cmd`, `sudo -u $user cmd`, a glob or brace value) gets the same verdict, naming that word, since shell-guard cannot tell which word the launcher runs; a value wholly in double quotes keeps the old reading. `ls | xargs $CMD` still runs, since the call passes no argument of its own.
