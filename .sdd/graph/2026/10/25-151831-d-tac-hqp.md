agentic-memory counts every character of a script written without spaces as one word: Han, Hiragana, Katakana, Thai, Lao, Khmer and Myanmar. Text in every other script counts by whitespace as before. One split serves every count: the body against `entry_max_words`, the title against 3-10 words in `new` and `retitle`, and the word sets the similar-entry hint compares, where each such character is a word of its own, exempt from the three-letter minimum.

- "Lieferschein export fails" counts 3 words.
- "納品書の出力が失敗" counts 9 words.
- "Fix 納品書 export" counts 5 words.

Why: `strings.Fields` reads a Japanese or Chinese body as one word, so the body limit never fires and every title reads as too short; the similar-entry hint's `[\p{L}\p{N}]{3,}` reads a whole sentence as one token, so two entries on the same subject never look alike. A character is the unit these scripts are written in, and counting it needs only the standard library's `unicode` tables.

Rejected: characters divided by two (a guess off in both directions, and a three-character title counts as two words and is flagged); dictionary segmentation (a dependency carrying per-language dictionaries, megabytes in every binary, for counts that only ever add a note and never refuse an entry).

Accepted costs: the count runs high, since a Chinese word averages about one and a half characters, so 500 words of Chinese is roughly 330 real words and a long body draws its note sooner; character-level word sets make the similar-entry hint noisier for those scripts than whole words would.

Acceptance criteria:

1. `words` in `internal/memory/render.go` counts each character of the listed scripts as one word and every other run by whitespace; a table-driven unit test covers Latin, Japanese, Chinese, Thai and mixed text, including the three lines above.
2. The body note, the title check in `new` and the title check in `retitle` use that count; a test files a Japanese title of 3 to 10 characters and gets no title note.
3. `wordSet` in `internal/memory/entries.go` yields each character of the listed scripts as its own word and keeps the three-letter minimum for every other script; a test shows two Japanese entries on one subject scoring above zero.
