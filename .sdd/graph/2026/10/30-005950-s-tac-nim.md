Agents choose which agentic-tools tool to take by its name and one-line summary, not by its usage line: in every real lookup measured on this machine, no take and no rejection turned on usage alone. Usage matters when the taken tool is run.

Source: Claude Code transcripts on the maintainer's machine, `~/.claude/projects/` and fdbox's archived autopilot transcripts, parsed for `agentic-tools` and older `pilot tools` `find`, `list` and `take` calls and the MCP equivalents. Of 37 lookups with hits, 18 came from sessions developing or maintaining the library and were left out; 19 remain (15 finds, 4 lists), all from fdbox document-migration work. The per-lookup record is attached as [usage-decision-summary.txt](./30-005950-s-tac-nim/usage-decision-summary.txt).

- **Picks.** 11 of 15 finds led to a take. In 6 the first hit was taken; in 4 only lower hits were. In all four lower-rank picks, comparing the skipped and taken hits' summaries, usage lines and the agent's query, the summaries alone named the different jobs in three; the fourth (`markdown-empty-headings` skipped for `docs-empty-headings`) differed in both summary and usage. The three lists that led to takes grabbed 19 tools in bulk from list output that printed no usage at all.
- **Reasoning.** Thinking text is mostly absent from transcripts (3,195 of 52,101 thinking blocks carry text, none in a real lookup), and takes usually follow the lookup within one to three calls with no text in between. Where text exists, 7 lookups cite summary-only words, 2 cite usage-only words that are generic ("base", "reading") or already in the agent's query, 10 cite neither.
- **Rejections.** 5 lookups led to no take; in none did the agent's text give an argument or input format as the reason.
- **Wrong picks.** No find was followed by a second take of a different tool. 2 of 14 single takes were never run; both were hedges taken beside a tool that was run, from find output that printed usage.
- **Run time.** Of 26 runs of a taken tool checked against its usage line, 21 matched its arguments and flags, 2 matched a later usage version, 1 used a flag found by reading the source, 2 could not be judged. 18 of 40 takes read the script before running it.

Estimate: about 0 worse picks per 100 finds without usage in hits, with an upper bound near 7 (the one ambiguous case of 15). Confidence is medium: the sample is small, from one project and one family of document tools, and silent reasoning cannot be seen; the lower-rank judgements are the measuring agent's.
