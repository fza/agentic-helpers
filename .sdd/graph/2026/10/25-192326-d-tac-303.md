agentic-memory keeps its post-compaction replay whole by nudging a seat to file as soon as its unfiled turns outgrow the replay, and says so in the replay whenever turns were still cut.

The replay is the condensed transcript since the seat's newest own entry, split into pieces of 9,000 characters (`pieceChars`) and capped at five (`maxPieces`) in `source/hooks/internal/memory/hooks.go`, because Claude Code inlines a hook's output only up to 10,000 characters and wires one `session-log <n>` hook per piece. `transcript.Pieces` keeps the newest five and drops the rest without a word. A seat that works for hours without filing loses its oldest turns at the next compaction, and nothing tells the session that they are gone. The existing nudge fires on context fill (65% and upward by rungs) or 400 KiB of transcript growth, neither of which tracks the replay's budget.

**Replay nudge.** The `UserPromptSubmit` hook (`prompt`) checks whether the condensed turns since the seat's newest own entry exceed `pieceChars * maxPieces`. When they do, it adds, once per newest entry:

`Turns since your newest entry no longer fit the post-compaction replay. File where work stands: findings, open questions, judgment calls. agentic-memory new --title '<3-10 words>' --topic <name>... < body`

Any entry the seat files moves the replay's start forward and re-arms the nudge. The ladder file records the newest-entry time it fired for (`replay_nudged_for`) and the transcript size at the last check (`replay_checked_size`); the check runs only once the transcript has grown by at least `pieceChars` bytes since then, so a prompt costs two `stat` calls until the transcript could hold a new piece. The replay nudge does not count toward the backstop: `Nudges` and the `Stop` backstop stay tied to the compaction nudge.

**Cut stated.** `transcript.Pieces` also returns how many older pieces it dropped. When it dropped any, the first `session-log` piece opens with:

`agentic-memory: the turns since seat <name>'s newest entry, in <n> pieces, newest last; <k> older pieces cut, the full transcript at <path>:`

and the header is unchanged when nothing was cut. The session reads the cut turns from the transcript only when it needs them.

Both strings are hook output and therefore contract: the `agent-memory` skill's "What comes back" section quotes the replay nudge, and `docs/memory/usage.md` and `docs/memory/architecture.md` describe the trigger and the cut header, in the same change.

Why: filing is what moves the replay's start, so the cheapest way to keep the replay whole is to ask for an entry at the moment it would stop fitting, before any compaction. A cut that must still happen is worth one line, since a session that does not know turns are missing trusts the compaction summary for them.

Rejected:
- Only stating the cut. Nothing prevents the loss, and recovering it means the agent reading a transcript file of many megabytes on its own initiative.
- Condensing older turns harder (question plus tool names only). Older turns lose their replies, and the grading rules become a new part to tune and test.
- Raising `maxPieces`. Every compaction puts more text back into the context it just freed, and a longer stretch still hits the cap.

Accepted costs: a long session hears one more kind of nudge; a prompt past the growth threshold condenses the transcript since the newest entry once; the skill and docs change with the hook wording.

Acceptance criteria:
- A seat whose condensed turns since its newest entry exceed `pieceChars * maxPieces` gets the replay nudge on the next prompt, exactly once until it files an entry.
- Filing any entry re-arms it; a seat whose turns fit never gets it.
- The replay nudge leaves `Nudges` and the `Stop` backstop untouched.
- Below the growth threshold the prompt hook does not read the transcript.
- A replay that drops pieces names the count and the transcript path in the first piece; one that drops none prints today's header.
- Tests cover each criterion, and each new guard is broken once to watch its test fail.
