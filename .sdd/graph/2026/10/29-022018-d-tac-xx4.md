A filing reply's similar-entry hint from `agentic-memory new` takes one line, `similar: <id> <title> (<score>)`, and a hit by meaning needs a score of at least 0.6. The hints are the similar-entry notes `new` has printed since agentic-memory shipped (20261025-014326-d-tac-mvn).

- **One line per hint.** The command lines suggesting "replaces it" (`supersede`) or "relates" (`link`) are gone from every reply. The skill core (20261029-021831-d-tac-93f) says once what to run: the same finding, `supersede <id> --by <new>`; related, `link <new> <id>`; otherwise nothing.
- **Floor 0.6.** Semantic hits must reach 0.6 on top of the existing mean + 2 deviations floor (`similarFloor` in `source/hooks/internal/memory/entries.go`). Term hits are unchanged.
- **JSON unchanged.** The `similar` array of the JSON answer keeps its shape.

Why: over three days and 1026 filings, hints were 62% of filing-reply characters (407k). Of 1064 hints, 225 (21%) were followed by a supersede, link or merge naming the id. By score band, hints shown / acted on: under 0.6, 101 / 4; 0.6 to 0.7, 442 / 76; 0.7 to 0.8, 432 / 115; 0.8 to 0.9, 71 / 29; 0.9 and above, 18 / 1. The command lines repeated on every hint what one skill line says once.

Rejected: one line at the current threshold (keeps 101 hints that were almost never acted on); floor 0.7 (drops 80 acted-on hints, a third of the useful ones).

Accepted costs: the agent writes the `supersede` or `link` call itself; 0.6 fits the current embedder and is measured again if the embedder changes.

Acceptance: a filing reply prints each hint as one line without command lines; a semantic hit under 0.6 is not printed even when it clears the deviation floor; the JSON `similar` array is unchanged; the agent-memory skill states what to run on a hint.
