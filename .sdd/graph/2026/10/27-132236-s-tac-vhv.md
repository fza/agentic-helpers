agentic-memory keeps entry vectors in immutable, memory-mapped segment files under `<cache>/<fp12>/segments/`, in the new package `internal/vectorseg`, in agentic-helpers commit f543637, not yet released. It closes the vector segments directive (20261027-130307-d-tac-wd3).

Tests in `internal/vectorseg` and `internal/memory` cover:

- a format round trip, an entry scoring by its best chunk;
- a torn segment skipped, then deleted with a stray temporary file by the next write;
- a row of an older content hash neither scored nor kept by a merge;
- a segment whose dead rows outnumber its live ones rewritten to its live rows;
- a duplicate row scored once and merged away;
- the segment count staying at or under 18 for 2,000 entries filed one at a time;
- a snapshot reading a segment deleted after it was taken;
- `vectors.gob` migrated into one segment with no embedder call, then deleted;
- a changed entry the only one embedded again.

Every guard was broken and watched failing, then restored; `go vet` and `go test -race ./...` pass. `docs/memory/architecture.md` describes the layout.

Deviations:

- Segments are not fsynced: a sync on darwin flushes the whole drive inside hook calls, as the cache's other files already skip it. A segment a crash left torn fails its size check, is skipped and deleted, and its entries are embedded again.
- A segment with no live row has no rule of its own: the dead-over-live rewrite covers it, writing nothing, and a separate rule could never fail a test.
- An entry whose body was dropped still scores by any row it has, as it did with `vectors.gob`, since nothing embeds it again.
