agentic-memory now reads a zombie or exiting `claude` process as gone, so such a husk no longer holds a memory seat, fixed in agentic-helpers commit a6bdc5d and released as v2.15.0. In gvb-mika, seat `main` stayed held by pid 58816, a `claude` in `ps` state `E` (inside exit) under a parent zsh also in `E`: the kernel read of 20261026-223829-d-tac-ah6 took the name and start time from `kern.proc.pid` but no state, so the husk still matched its claim, and `claim main` and `who` both treated it as running.

`internal/process` now returns no process for a darwin `kinfo_proc` with `P_stat` zombie (5) or `P_flag` `P_WEXIT` (0x2000), and for a Linux `/proc/<pid>/stat` in state `Z` or `X`, in both the parent-and-name and the start-time reads, so seat liveness and the owning-process walk skip it. `docs/memory/architecture.md` names the rule.

Verified: table-driven tests of the darwin flag check and of procfs fixtures in states `Z` and `X`, each guard broken once and caught; the darwin read checked against the live husk (pid 58816 reads as gone, the test process itself as running); and the released v2.15.0 `agentic-memory holder main` in gvb-mika answers `main is free` where 2.12.2 answers it held by pid 58816.

The darwin wiring around `SysctlKinfoProc` is reached by no test, since the kernel read cannot be faked; the live checks above stand in for one.
