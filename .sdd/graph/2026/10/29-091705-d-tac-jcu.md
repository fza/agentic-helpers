agentic-memory gets no session-end wrap-up: no background LLM run turns a closed session's unfiled turns into memory entries or a state, and the next claim keeps replaying the raw last turns from Claude Code's transcript, read in place as the agentic-memory directive (20261025-014326-d-tac-mvn) set out. The rejection applies the token-cost criteria (20261029-021515-d-tac-00q): least risk first, then fewest tokens.

**The rejected design.** When a session closes with more than about 5000 characters of turns since its seat's newest write, a detached `claude -p` run (Haiku or Sonnet at low or medium effort, with another, cheaper model as an option) wraps up the transcript into memory entries, so the next claim replays entries instead of turns. It runs isolated with `--safe-mode`, which turns off instruction files, skills, plugins, hooks and MCP servers and works on a subscription login, as sdd's own `claude -p` runner does; `--bare` was not the isolation path, since it authenticates only with an API key.

**Why it is rejected.**
- *It saves little.* A claim replays a mean of 4170 characters of the previous session's last turns (median 2247, p90 13869, 42 claims machine-wide); only claims over 5000 characters would change, saving 1.3k to 3.3k main-model tokens once per such claim.
- *Stale turns become standing entries.* Test runs over the six longest slices wrote accurate entries and invented no identifier, but both models filed a decision the turns reported as current, "json key removal postponed to v3.0.0, do not do it before then", as a lasting entry, although the graph has overturned it since (20261029-083737-d-tac-kib). Replayed turns read as a dated record of the last session, read once; an entry returns in recall and briefs for days as if it held, which is the mistake the risk criterion rules out.
- *Entries cost on every later brief.* Each wrap-up filed four to nine entries, which later briefs carry for days, likely more tokens than the one-time replay they replace.
- *Lossy.* The longest slice kept six to eight of its 32 exact identifiers in the entries; the specifics a next session acts on are in the raw turns.
- *A cheaper third-party model publishes transcript content* to another provider, credentials included where a transcript holds one.

**Alternatives also rejected.** A wrap-up writing only the seat's state, filing no entries, avoids lingering stale entries but still drops most identifiers and adds a detached runner racing the next claim, for the same small saving. Shrinking the replay budgets instead was measured too: the post-compaction replay (mean 10770 characters, never cut) carries 62% of its exact identifiers that neither Claude Code's summary nor its verbatim-kept messages hold, so a smaller budget drops what the next turn needs.

The measurements and the test-run setup are in [wrapup-measurements.md](./29-091705-d-tac-jcu/wrapup-measurements.md). Settled: nothing is built, and the replay stays as it is.
