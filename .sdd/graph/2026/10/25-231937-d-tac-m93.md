agentic-memory entries belong to a shelf, and every seat files onto one shelf. A seat has a shelf of its own unless it is put on another, so a store where no seat shares behaves as today; several seats on one shelf read and write one memory, as concurrent agents working one board row do.

Why: agents running side by side on one job need one memory between them, while each still holds its own seat (one session at a time) and every entry still records which seat filed it.

## What

- **The noun is `shelf`.** A seat maps to exactly one shelf; a shelf holds any number of seats.
- **An entry belongs to a shelf** and records the seat that filed it.
- **`claim <seat> --shelf <name>` puts a seat on a shelf when the seat is first claimed**, and the seat stays on it for good. A claim of an existing seat naming another shelf is refused (`<seat> is on shelf <name>`); naming its own shelf, or no shelf, claims as usual. A seat first claimed without `--shelf` gets a shelf of its own. MCP: key `shelf` on `claim`. `--shelf` combines with `--session`.
- **A shelf comes into being when a first seat names it.** Shelf names follow the seat-name rule, and no two shelves share a name. A seat's own shelf carries the seat's name; a seat first claimed without `--shelf` whose name a shelf already carries is refused (`<name> names a shelf; claim it with --shelf <name>`). `rename` renames the seat and, where its shelf still carries the seat's old name, the shelf too; a new name another shelf carries is refused.
- **`claim --shelf <name>` without a seat name mints one**: the next free `<shelf>-<n>` (`main-2`), drawn and claimed under the store lock, so two sessions joining at once never collide. MCP: `claim` with `shelf` and no `name`.
- **The `agent-memory` skill maps the user's words onto a claim without asking wherever one reading fits.** "main" (in any language) claims seat `main`, as before. "main, shared":
  - `main` exists and is free → claim `main`;
  - `main` exists and is held → `claim --shelf <main's shelf>`, a minted seat beside it;
  - `main` is new and the store holds exactly one shelf → `claim main --shelf <that shelf>`;
  - `main` is new and several shelves exist, or none → ask the user which shelf, naming them.
- **Mapping.** Each `seats.json` entry gains `"shelf": "<shelf id>"`, a shelf id being `h-` and 6 characters; every shelf has a name, its follows and its lifetime. An entry's shelf is its filing seat's, since a seat never changes shelf, so no event names a shelf and no event kind is added. Where shelves, their logs and their settings lie on disk is a directive of its own.

- **Every seat on a shelf may `retire`, `retain`, `retitle` and `tag` the shelf's entries.** The shelf owns them; each entry still shows the seat that filed it, and each event the seat that made it. A seat on another shelf is refused: `<id> is on shelf <name>`. A seat's own shelf holds one seat, so nothing changes outside shared shelves.
- **Every other seat on the shelf is told of such a change**, on the line `agentic-memory: changed on shelf <name> (<n>):`, one line per entry naming the verb and the seat that made it.
- **The seats on a shelf are its subscribers.** Both a shelf mate's new entry and a change to one reach them through the machinery that delivers `--for` and followed-topic arrivals today: seen marks, the prompt and PostToolUse hooks, the idle Stop waiter. No second notification path exists for shelves.

- **A seat's memory is its shelf.** Brief, search and prompt recall treat every entry on the seat's shelf as the seat's own, beside the entries others filed for it. A shelf mate's new entry reaches the other seats on the shelf as an arrival, on the existing `agentic-memory: new for <seat> (<n>):` line. `search --seat <s>` narrows to one filer; `--all-seats` keeps its meaning.

- **`--for` and `follow` attach to shelves.** `--for <name>` takes a seat or a shelf name and delivers to a shelf: a shelf name to that shelf, a seat name to the seat's shelf, so `--for` on a store where no seat shares delivers as before. `--for all` keeps its meaning. Follows are stored with the shelf: a topic one seat on a shelf follows, every seat on it receives.

## Rejected

- **A notification path of its own for shelf mates.** It would duplicate what seen marks and the arrival hooks already do for `--for` and followed topics, and the two would drift.
- **`--for` delivering to the shelf while `follow` stays per seat.** An entry would show to one seat on a shelf and not to its shelf mates: a second scope inside one shared memory.
- **`--for` and `follow` naming seats only.** Reaching a row would mean naming every seat on it, and an agent joining the row later would miss what went to the others.
- **Search and recall over the shelf, the brief's working set over the seat's own filings.** Two scopes to explain, and a shelf mate's note would rank below one's own however hot.
- **The seat's own filings by default, a `--shelf` flag widening to the shelf.** The shared memory would be shared only where every agent remembered the flag.
- **The filer keeping its entries while its seat is held, the shelf after.** Whether the same command succeeds would depend on whether another agent still runs at that moment.
- **Only the filer, as before.** A finished job's live notes on a row still being worked could only be superseded, never retired, and would keep filling the row's brief.
- **A `shelf <name>` subcommand moving the held seat.** One more subcommand, and a moved seat's filings stay behind on the old shelf, split from its new ones.
- **Shelves made on their own first** (`shelf new <name>`), seats joining only existing ones. Catches a typo, but costs every board row an extra step, and leaves an empty shelf standing until a seat joins.
- **`booth`.** Stays inside the seating metaphor, but playful in error text.
- **`locker`.** A locker reads as private, so sharing one would be the odd case.
- **`crew`.** Names the seats, not the memory, and a one-seat crew, the default, reads oddly.
- **`scope`.** Claude Code already says user and project scope, and `search` uses scope for its seat filter.
- **`vault`** suggests secrets; **`ledger`** and **`manifest`** name the log; **`pool`** implies always shared; **`table`** makes `table row-7ah` read as a data table's row.

- **The skill picking the new seat's name** (listing the seats, then the next free `main-<n>`). Two sessions joining at once could pick the same name, the second refused and retrying.
- **Asking the user for every shared seat's name.** A question each time someone says "shared", where the store already decides it.
- **A shelf id stamped into every event.** It would rewrite the log and add a field every older binary ignores, for a mapping `seats.json` already fixes.

## Accepted costs

- One seat on a shared shelf can retire what another filed; the event names who did.
- A busy row's brief holds every agent's entries, ordered by heat.
- No single seat on a shared shelf can be addressed alone by `--for`, and one seat's `follow` changes what its shelf mates receive.
- A seat put on the wrong shelf is fixed only by claiming a new seat.

## Acceptance criteria

- On a store where no seat shares a shelf, brief, search, recall, `--for`, `follow` and the ownership checks answer exactly as before.
- `claim <seat> --shelf <name>` puts a new seat on that shelf, creating it where no shelf carries the name; a later claim of the seat naming another shelf is refused and changes nothing; `--shelf` together with `--session` reserves the seat on that shelf.
- A new seat claimed without `--shelf` under a name a shelf carries is refused.
- Two seats on one shelf: each sees the other's entries in brief, search and prompt recall; `search --seat` narrows to one filer; each line still names the filing seat.
- A seat on the shelf may retire, retain, retitle and tag a shelf mate's entry, and the other seats on the shelf get `agentic-memory: changed on shelf <name> (<n>):`; a seat on another shelf is refused with `<id> is on shelf <name>`.
- A shelf mate's new entry reaches the other seats on the shelf through seen marks, the prompt and PostToolUse hooks and the idle Stop waiter, as `--for` arrivals do.
- `--for <seat>` delivers to that seat's shelf, `--for <shelf>` to the shelf, `--for all` to every shelf; a topic one seat on a shelf follows reaches every seat on it.
- `claim --shelf main` with no seat name claims `main-<n>`, the lowest free `n`, on shelf `main`; two such claims at once get two seats.
- The skill states the "<seat>, shared" mapping above, and asks only where several shelves, or none, could be meant.
- `rename` renames a seat's own shelf with it and refuses a name another shelf carries.
- `docs/memory/` and the `agent-memory` skill describe shelves, `claim --shelf` and the new notice line in the same change.
- Every new guard is broken and watched failing on its own test, the file restored byte-identically.
