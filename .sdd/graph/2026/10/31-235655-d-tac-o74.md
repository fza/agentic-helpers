agentic-memory keeps a store-wide high-water mark of the newest time it has stored and never reads or writes a time earlier than it, so a system clock set back leaves ordering intact: memory's clock is the later of the system clock and one nanosecond past the mark. Today every stored time comes from the system clock as it stands, and a clock set back makes everything stored before look as if it lay in the future until real time passes it.

**What it fixes.** Entries and events filed after a rollback still sort after everything filed before, so new-since checks (other seats' new and changed entries in the brief, `who` filed and used counts) see them. Heat no longer treats an entry as never decaying because its filing lies ahead of the clock. Nudges and the state request, which compare stored times, no longer repeat. The reservation window, a temporary seat's retention and the 14-day `revive` window no longer stretch by the rollback.

**Where.** One function, `mem.now()`, answers the later of the system clock and the mark, and every place that stamps or compares a time goes through it: `brief.go`, `who.go`, `due.go`, `ends.go`, `cache.go`, `hooks.go`, `shipped.go`, `purge.go`, `lifecycle.go` in `source/hooks/internal/memory/`. The mark lives in the store beside its log and rises with every stamp written; a store holding none starts from the newest stamp in its log.

**Not covered.** While the system clock trails the mark, memory's time stands at the mark: due times and `at:` ends fire late by up to the rollback, and decay pauses for that span. Content-hash anchors (`changed:`) are unaffected either way, since they compare hashes, not times. A clock set forward and then corrected leaves the mark ahead, which reads the same as a rollback of that span.

**Rejected.** Accepting the effects and recording them only: every effect heals on its own, but ordering errors in the brief mislead agents for the whole span. Dropping the analysis of these effects without recording it: the next rollback needs the same analysis again.

**Accepted costs.** Changes in about ten places, each a chance of a new defect, guarded by tests driving `mem.now()` with a clock set back. A store-wide mark written on every stamp.

**Acceptance.**
- With the clock set back a day after filing, an entry filed then sorts after the earlier ones, and another seat's brief lists it as new.
- Heat is never computed from a negative age: an entry filed before the rollback ranks by the age the mark gives it.
- A nudge answered before the rollback does not repeat after it.
- `docs/memory/architecture.md` states the mark and what it does not cover.
