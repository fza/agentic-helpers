Two sessions of one project can both be told they hold the same agentic-memory seat, while only one claim file survives; the other session's later writes land under no seat. Expected, per the claim force flag removal (20261025-164209-d-tac-qj7) and the reservation rules of claim for another session (20261025-223640-d-tac-d2p): nothing takes a seat, held or reserved, from a session that still runs, and a claim either holds the seat or is refused.

Observed, by reading `internal/memory/seats.go`, `claimfor.go`, `hooks.go` and `who.go` and reproducing each path in tests that drive two sessions against one store:

- **No lock around a claim.** `claim` reads the seat's claim file, judges its holder, then removes and writes it as separate steps. Two sessions claiming one free seat at once both pass the check and both write; both get a brief, the last write wins. `claim --session` reservations race the same way.
- **A long-running MCP server keeps the seat registry of its first call.** The `memory` MCP server caches `seats.json` and `shelves.json` for its whole life. A seat another session mints after that call has no key in the cached registry, so a `claim` of it finds no claim file to judge and overwrites the live holder's claim. A seat renamed since is claimed by its old name, and a shelf made since has no name in `who`.
- **Other claim writers judge apart from writing.** `who` freeing a gone holder's claim, `release` of a seat whose holder is gone, SessionEnd's close, and a hook re-pointing a claim at a new session id or transcript each read, judge and write separately: a claim another session wrote in between is removed or overwritten, and a hook running beside its own session's `release` writes the released claim back.

The deviation sits at the claim file: it is changed by many writers with no common lock and, over MCP, judged against a stale registry, so the rule that a running holder keeps its seat holds only when no two writers overlap.
